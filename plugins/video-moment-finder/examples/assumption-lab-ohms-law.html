Generated what-if case

Double the voltage, keep the condition

Applied voltage: 4 V
Fixed resistance: 100 ohms

Current = 40 mA

I = V/R = 4/100 A = 0.04 A = 40 mA. This is twice the reference current because R did not change.

Within the stated model. Doubling current follows only while resistance stays fixed.

Assumptions in this case

  • The element obeys V = IR.
  • Resistance stays at 100 ohms despite the changed voltage.

Reasoning

  • Both operating points use the same denominator, 100 ohms.
  • Therefore I₂/I₁ = V₂/V₁ = 2 within this model.

Still unknown

  • The arithmetic does not establish whether a real resistor can sustain this operating point.

A known voltage with an unknown final resistance

Applied voltage: 2 V
Final resistance: unknown

Exact current is undetermined

Even retaining V = IR at the new operating point, I = 2/R_final A needs the final resistance. The initial 100 ohms is insufficient.

The source does not settle this. The provided evidence cannot supply a numerical heated current.

Assumptions in this case

  • The element still obeys V = IR at each operating point.
  • Heating changes resistance, and no final value is supplied.

Reasoning

  • An equation with an unknown denominator does not yield a unique numerical current.
  • No sign or size of the resistance change is specified; do not invent one.

Still unknown

Generated what-if case

Doubling voltage no longer fixes the ratio

Applied voltage: 4 V
Final resistance: unknown

Exact current is undetermined

I = 4/R_final A. Compared with the fixed 2 V reference, the ratio is 200/R_final when R_final is in ohms. It equals two only when R_final = 100 ohms.

The source does not settle this. The two-times current conclusion requires the very assumption removed in this case.

Assumptions in this case

  • The element still obeys V = IR at this operating point.
  • The new resistance differs from 100 ohms by an unspecified amount.

Reasoning

  • Divide 4/R_final A by the reference current 0.02 A to obtain the dimensionless current ratio.
  • A changed denominator prevents inferring a two-times ratio from voltage alone.

Still unknown

  • The final resistance, exact current, and direction of the resistance change.
  • Whether any proposed physical component tolerates the operating point.
