01 / Generated explanation · check the assumptions

Multiply matching coordinates, then add

For two real two-dimensional vectors a=(a₁,a₂) and b=(b₁,b₂), the dot product is a₁b₁+a₂b₂.

With a=(3,4) and b=(2,0), only the horizontal contribution remains: 3×2+4×0=6. The result is a scalar, not a new vector.

02 / Generated explanation · check the assumptions

Read the sign as alignment

For nonzero vectors, a·b=|a||b|cos(θ). A positive dot product means an acute angle; zero means a right angle; a negative result means an obtuse angle.

The unit coordinate vectors (1,0) and (0,1) make a right angle, and 1×0+0×1=0. The coordinate rule and geometric interpretation agree.

03 / Generated explanation · check the assumptions

Check the assumption before using the rule

Reversing b from (2,0) to (−2,0) changes the example's dot product from 6 to −6. It points in the opposite direction.

A zero dot product alone does not establish a defined 90-degree angle: if either vector is zero, the dot product is zero but its direction and angle are undefined. The nonzero assumption matters.

Keep these ideas

What to take with you

  • A dot product is a scalar obtained by multiplying matching coordinates and adding.
  • For nonzero vectors, the sign describes whether their angle is acute, right, or obtuse.
  • Check the nonzero assumption before turning zero dot product into an angle claim.
