02 / concept

Is the dot product of two vectors itself a vector?

Reveal answer

No. The dot product is a scalar, even though its inputs are vectors.

Generated practice · see the reasoning

Generated reasoning. The coordinate definition adds real products and therefore returns one real number.

03 / concept

Can a real vector have a negative dot product with itself?

Get a hint

Expand (a, b) · (a, b).

Reveal answer

No. It is a sum of squares, so it is nonnegative.

Generated practice · see the reasoning

Generated reasoning. a²+b² is at least zero for real a and b; it is zero only for the zero vector.

For two nonzero real vectors, what does a zero dot product imply about their directions?

Reveal answer

They are perpendicular. The nonzero condition avoids assigning a direction to the zero vector.

s-definition · synthetic

00:00–00:25

For real two-dimensional vectors u=(a,b) and v=(c,d), define u·v=ac+bd. The result is a scalar.

s-sign · synthetic

00:25–00:50

The dot product of a vector with itself is the sum of its squared coordinates, so it is nonnegative. For nonzero real vectors, a zero dot product indicates perpendicular directions.

s-example · synthetic

00:50–01:10

For u=(2,-1) and v=(3,4), u·v=2×3+(-1)×4=6-4=2.

s-scale · synthetic

01:10–01:30

Scaling one vector by a real factor k scales its dot product by k: (ku)·v=k(u·v).
